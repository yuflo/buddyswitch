# PMF Recon Framework

This skill is optimized for **small teams, solo founders, indie hackers, and bootstrapped builders** who need fast, evidence-led validation.

## Core decision questions

1. **Problem intensity**: Is the problem painful, frequent, and costly enough?
2. **Segment sharpness**: Which narrow user group seems to feel it most strongly?
3. **Alternative pressure**: What are people already doing to solve it?
4. **Adoption feasibility**: Is there a realistic path to early usage or early payment?
5. **Next validation move**: What is the cheapest next test that reduces uncertainty?

## Practical validation loop

### A. Hypothesis frame
Before searching, write down:
- who you think the user is
- what problem you think they have
- where the problem shows up in their workflow
- what they use today instead
- what change in behavior or payment would validate the idea

### B. Pain evidence collection
Look for evidence that users:
- complain repeatedly about the same issue
- describe the pain in concrete terms
- reveal time, money, quality, or emotional costs
- use ugly workarounds or stitched-together workflows
- request missing features again and again

### C. Alternative mapping
For every promising pain point, identify:
- incumbent software
- spreadsheets / manual processes
- agencies / freelancers / human assistants
- internal tooling
- doing nothing / accepting the pain

### D. Segment narrowing
Avoid broad labels like:
- marketers
- developers
- creators
- operations teams

Push toward narrower wedges like:
- solo B2B SaaS founders doing founder-led outbound
- agency operators managing client reporting manually
- Shopify sellers producing multilingual listings without in-house translators
- small design studios handing off repetitive QA to junior staff

### E. Decision threshold
A problem becomes more promising when you see several of these together:
- multiple independent mentions of the same pain
- concrete costs or consequences
- existing spend or heavy time investment
- dissatisfaction with current alternatives
- easy-to-reach early segment
- a realistic first-step product or service test

## Recommended output stance

Do **not** say “there is clearly PMF” from desk research alone.

Say things like:
- there is **strong evidence of pain** in this segment
- there is **moderate evidence of dissatisfaction with alternatives**
- there is **weak evidence of willingness to pay**
- the next best move is **5 user interviews** or **a concierge test**

## Suggested research depths

### Quick
Use when the user wants a fast go/no-go signal.
- 2–3 source categories
- 8–12 evidence items
- 1–2 candidate ICPs
- 1 concise decision memo

### Standard
Use for most idea validation tasks.
- 3–5 source categories
- 15–25 evidence items
- stronger alternative mapping
- clearer recommended next experiment

### Deep
Use when the idea is expensive, regulated, crowded, or technically heavy.
- broader competitor scan
- more review mining
- more segmentation work
- stronger emphasis on unknowns and risks
